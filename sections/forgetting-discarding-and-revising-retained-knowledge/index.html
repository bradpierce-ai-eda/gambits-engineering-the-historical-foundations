Recall after an instruction to forget#

Robert Bjork’s 1972 chapter distinguishes instructions given during a sequence, after a set, or item by item. In the latter arrangement the cue can coincide with presentation or follow it. He separates two questions about the effect of such a cue. One concerns how much of the material designated for remembering is subsequently recalled. The other concerns how much of the material designated for forgetting remains accessible on recall or recognition tests. An instruction can reduce interference with what is to be remembered while leaving some of the other material accessible. [T07-W001]

Kluge and Gronau examine retrieval cues in organizations. Their proposal concerns an old routine that still comes to mind because familiar tools, records or circumstances cue it. Changing those cues while introducing conditions for the new routine is a way to change which practice is prompted in everyday work. [T07-W002]

Written procedures and work that people continue to do#

Martin de Holan and Phillips studied seven Cuban hotels through visits, documents and 78 interviews. Five hotels were associated with alliances and two were comparison properties. The authors distinguish failure to retain a new practice, decay of an established practice, and deliberately abandoning an adopted arrangement. Their Withwind account describes manuals and training specifying new procedures while managers repeatedly intervened to keep everyday practice aligned with them. The manager’s example of kitchen staff substituting ingredients illustrates how an old way of working could remain available. [T07-W003]

At Caribbean Hotel, management adopted another hotel’s structure and introduced corresponding operating and communication procedures. The manager reported that procedures went unused unless checked by the same level that had imposed them. Meetings were taking time without solving the problems. The organization changed its structure and routines. The authors discuss this as abandonment of an innovation. Their separate comparison of food and beverage costs per guest in three hotels concerns deterioration of an established practice. They relate it to turnover and dependence on supervision. Those hotels were the subset that could supply complete cost records. [T07-W003]

Withdrawing support#

The truth-maintenance system described in S60 concerns another kind of retention. Doyle’s 1979 memo explains how the current support for a proposition can change while the stored statement and its reasons remain available. The system can withdraw a conclusion without losing the record needed to reconsider it. The object being revised is the current set of supported conclusions. [CM-W003]
