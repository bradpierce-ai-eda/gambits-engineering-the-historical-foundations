S70 Learning and practising creative methods #

Part of Gambits Engineering: The Historical Foundations (Brad Pierce, 2026). No independent publication identifier.

Subsection linksWhat creativity training improvesArranging interventions for childrenPlacing exercises within an online courseDifferent levels of creative accomplishment

A creative procedure can be taught as a sequence of actions, practised in a particular setting and assessed through the work that follows. Training studies examine these connections at several levels. Their accounts range from the effects measured across many programmes to the placement of an exercise within a proposed course, and from a learner's personally meaningful discovery to professional achievement.

What creativity training improves#

Ut Na Sio and Hugues Lortie-Forgues reviewed five decades of creativity-training research in 2024. Their principal meta-analysis included 169 studies, 844 effect sizes and 18,486 participants. The authors distinguished cognitive content, such as idea-generation skills, from content directed at motivation, emotion or personality, with a further category for combined programmes. Outcomes included divergent-thinking tasks, problem solving and creative products. [CRE-W041, method and results]

The unadjusted average training effect was 0.53 standard deviations. The review also found evidence consistent with publication bias and applied several corrections. PEESE, trim-and-fill, robust Bayesian meta-analysis and the top-ten-percent approach produced estimates around 0.29 to 0.32 standard deviations. The PET estimate was smaller and statistically uncertain. The authors report a positive average effect under several adjustments while showing how substantially its estimated size depends on accounting for the evidence available. [CRE-W041, publication-bias analyses]

The review examined the contents and duration of training as well as average effects. Cognitive and combined programmes compared favorably with the smaller non-cognitive category; the analysis did not establish a difference between cognitive and combined content. A positive association with duration in the simpler analysis weakened when other characteristics were considered together. These findings give more information than an undifferentiated claim that creativity can be taught. [CRE-W041, moderator analyses]

Sio and Lortie-Forgues also examined study quality and the concentration of outcomes in particular kinds of task. Their account treats measured improvement, the content of instruction and the quality of its evaluation as connected questions. A programme teaching generation skills and assessed with a divergent-thinking task supplies evidence about that accomplishment. Its result is most informative when the reader knows what was practised and what the assessment asked participants to do. [CRE-W041, discussion]

Arranging interventions for children#

Patrícia Alves-Oliveira and colleagues' review of children's creativity interventions organized programmes at eight levels: cognitive processing skill, training technique, delivery medium, practice exercise, target, setting, administrator and dimension of creativity. A skill such as flexibility is distinct from a technique used to develop it, and that technique is distinct from its delivery through discussion, programmed instruction or a digital environment. The classification allows these parts of an intervention to be examined together. [CRE-W042, taxonomy]

The reviewed programmes include imaginative exercises, expressive activity, elaboration, cooperative learning and movement. A teacher can give sequential instructions, arrange discussion or ask learners to work together; an activity can use familiar materials or a computer or robotic partner. These are accounts of the programmes assembled in the review. Their arrangement makes the people conducting the

teaching and the circumstances of practice visible alongside the named creative technique. [CRE-W042, results and discussion]

The review distinguishes the dimension assessed from the activity used for teaching. In its classification, assessments concentrated on the person, commonly through tests: approximately 83 percent, compared with 9 percent directed at process and 8 percent at product. An intervention may involve producing something together while its evaluation chiefly measures individual performance on another task. This distinction helps explain what the accumulated evidence describes. [CRE-W042, assessment of creativity]

The authors' eight-level account also allows a method to be adapted without treating every adaptation as an entirely new technique. Delivery, exercises and teaching personnel can change while a programme continues to address a related skill. The review provides a way of describing these arrangements and their diversity across children's interventions. [CRE-W042]

Placing exercises within an online course#

Viviane Kuntz and colleagues proposed an arrangement of creative techniques for massive open online courses in 2015. They related particular operations to occasions in a course: welcoming participants, introducing people and the platform, forming groups, establishing plans and working on content. The chapter's procedure is to ask what an activity could contribute at each point, rather than add an isolated creativity exercise to an otherwise unchanged course. [CRE-W043, pp. 217–227]

An opening combinatorial activity asks learners to imagine another form or structure for the subject. Unusual questions can change an introductory exchange. Exploring the positive, negative and interesting aspects of a platform gives participants an activity through which to consider the environment they will use. Profile-based games can help form groups. These proposals connect generating alternatives with the practical work of entering a shared course. Word-based recreation can explore another organization of the course content. [CRE-W043, table 7]

For planning, the chapter brings a desired future into comparison with the current situation and considers inviting someone unfamiliar with the subject to introduce another perspective. For work on content, a mind map can support individual interpretation, inversion can break down and rearrange a logical structure, and unusual analogy can connect material that initially seems unrelated. Each technique is assigned a teaching occasion and an intended contribution. [CRE-W043, tables 4 and 7]

The chapter concludes by proposing a pilot course to examine this arrangement. It is therefore an instructional design and rationale, with concrete adaptations available to inspect. Its historical contribution lies in the proposed connection between established creative operations and the structure of an online course. [CRE-W043, conclusion]

Different levels of creative accomplishment#

Agustín Tristán López and Liliana Mendoza González's 2016 review of creativity taxonomies considers the range of accomplishments that education and assessment can address. Their account of the four-C model distinguishes personally meaningful discovery, everyday creativity, professional creativity and historically influential achievement. These levels give a place to work that is creative for the learner without requiring it to transform a field. [CRE-W044, four-C model]

Their examples make the distinctions concrete. A preschool child's drawing can embody a personally significant discovery. A child with some instruction in sculpture may produce an independent work worth displaying. An engineer's product can solve a client's problem and receive recognition within professional practice. A major work in music, art, technology or science can alter a domain much more widely. The

review uses these cases to explain why the expectations appropriate to one achievement need not fit another. [CRE-W044]

The authors also distinguish an unfolding process from a finished product. Observation and recorded sessions can help reconstruct how an activity developed; expert judgment can address qualities of completed work. Choreography, a technical device and a mathematical contribution pose different problems of description and evaluation. The review examines such differences while seeking more explicit accounts of creative qualities. [CRE-W044, assessment and creative-process taxonomies]

These distinctions give learning and practice several legitimate objects. A programme can develop a person's ways of approaching material, improve performance on a task or support a valued product. The studies and proposals in this section specify these accomplishments in different ways, providing historical accounts of what people have tried to teach and how they have recognized a result. [CRE-W041; CRE-W042; CRE-W043; CRE-W044]
