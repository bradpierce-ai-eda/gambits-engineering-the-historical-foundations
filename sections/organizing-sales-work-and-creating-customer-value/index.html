From seven selling steps to continuing organizational functions#

Moncrief and Marshall contrast the traditional seven selling steps with a changed organization of sales work. Prospecting, preapproach, approach, presentation, handling objections, closing and follow-up organize a familiar sequence. Their evolving functions include customer retention and relinquishment, knowledge and database management, relationship development, marketing the product, problem solving, adding value and relationship maintenance. These functions need not occur once each in a fixed order. [SL-W002]

Their argument follows changes in who performs an activity and what supports it. Prospecting can be supplied by another unit; preparation can use shared account records rather than an individual’s index cards; a team can work with several members of a buying centre. Problem solving and continuing service can span meetings and participants. The resulting account concerns an ongoing customer relationship and division of work, rather than simply renaming stages in a single conversation. [SL-W002]

Situational, functional and process accounts of sales management#

Руденко distinguishes situational, functional and process approaches to sales management. A situational approach attends to the particular conditions under which selling takes place. A functional approach makes responsibilities and management activities visible. A process approach follows connected work across those functions. Each directs attention toward a different object of management, from an encountered condition to an assigned responsibility or a sequence that crosses departmental boundaries. [SL-W016]

In the functional account, planning, organization, motivation and control organize management responsibilities. The process account instead follows market information through connected transformations and supporting activities toward results and satisfied needs. A situational response may solve an immediate issue without specifying that continuing whole. Rudenko’s comparison consequently concerns how a sales operation is managed, with conversation techniques located within a larger set of linked activities. [SL-W016]

Understanding a customer’s business and shaping a valuable offer#

Terho, Haas, Eggert and Ulaga conceptualize value-based selling through understanding the customer’s business model, crafting a value proposition and communicating customer value. Understanding includes earning logic and operating facts, not just requested specifications. Crafting uses that knowledge to shape an offering; communication makes the proposed difference credible through calculations, comparisons, references or tests. Their qualitative investigation draws on eleven purposefully selected experienced firms, senior informants and 176 transcript pages. [SL-W019]

The truck illustration begins with a request for twenty vehicles. Investigation of materials, distances, time and maintenance requirements supports a proposal for eighteen. The seller changes the proposed fleet by understanding the work it must perform, rather than merely reducing the price of the requested twenty. Capital and operating implications become part of explaining value, alongside the reported continuing relationship with the customer. [SL-W019]

The dairy illustration follows washing frequency and the use of water, energy, chemicals and time. Information about the process and relevant plant experience supports an estimate of a saving opportunity. Tests or a savings-sharing arrangement can connect that estimate to a commercial proposal.

Information access, calculation and the form of commitment thus remain together in the account. The authors distinguish their explicit concern with customer business value from related constructs such as adaptation, consultation and partnering. [SL-W019]

The scope of becoming a solution provider#

Evers and colleagues’ solution-provider report joins the scope of an offering to its realization, selling and management. A product/service combination can involve a different allocation of responsibility rather than a new label on an existing bundle. The three compass categories direct attention to how a chosen solution scope is made workable across these activities. [SL-W007] Its distinction among solution scopes moves from support for a customer’s process steps to taking responsibility for subprocesses and, at the broadest scope, results. The report treats these as different commitments, rather than assuming that the largest scope is always preferable. [SL-W007]

Their comparison of consultative, insight and value selling is one classification of the approaches, including their placement of SPIN and Challenger. The Siemens ShareNet discussion gives shared lessons and knowledge a supporting role across solution work. Making such information available concerns the organization behind the seller, as well as the skills brought to an encounter. [SL-W007] In the ShareNet illustration, project participants review the work and its results, record success and failure factors, and make them searchable by other departments before a similar project begins. The practical connection is between a completed project’s interpretation and preparation elsewhere in the firm. [SL-W007]

Proposal approval and the handoff into project delivery#

Cooper and Winters connect selling services with defining and delivering the promised work. Their six iterative stages move through opportunity, initial need and vision, a detailed feasible roadmap, a formal solution, contract and execution. Pursuit strategy, opportunity validation, internal proposal review and project kickoff supply four checkpoints. Continuing opportunity strategy, project oversight and account planning accompany the stages. [SL-W022]

Internal proposal review brings delivery authority into the decision before an external promise. The project manager can withhold a signature where feasibility is unacceptable; discussion can also lead management to accept an identified risk explicitly. The arrangement makes the connection between a commercial commitment and its execution a decision to be taken, rather than a handoff assumed to work by itself. [SL-W022]

Their e-commerce contrast explains why involvement can differ with scope. Helping define an entire venture, its business and its systems requires interdisciplinary development of a feasible vision. A later bounded catalogue assignment can be much more specified. The nine-box conversation they discuss can span several people and meetings: it is a guide to developing understanding and agreement, rather than a dialogue that must fit in one call. [SL-W022]

Ordering, settlement and a wholesaler’s selling time#

粟島浩二’s industrial-parts wholesaler case connects changes in ordering and delivery with changes in settlement and sales work. Customers could order around the clock and receive flexible small deliveries. Automated, consolidated settlement and quicker collection reduced routine administrative demands on the sales function. Released attention could then be directed to new customers. The arrangement depended on the commercial and financial process as well as the convenience of delivery. [SL-W017]

Specialization in power components also changed who could become a customer. Firms that might otherwise have been competitors could buy from the wholesaler, with goods shipped directly to their

own buyers. The case thus concerns both the handling of existing transactions and a changed role within a trading network. The author goes on to propose further links involving research and development, quality coordination and incentives; these proposals extend the argument beyond the reported company arrangement. [SL-W017]

The buyer’s progress and the seller’s process#

西川功一’s DSPM and cloud-CRM account records the buyer’s stage separately from the seller’s process. Completing a seller task does not establish that the buyer has progressed. Keeping the two axes distinct makes a mismatch visible and supplies a question about what action might help the buyer move forward. [SL-W015]

The procedure then follows an action with observation and revision. A candidate response is tried, subsequent buyer progress examined and the process standard adjusted. Modifiable CRM fields and tests let frontline participants revise the arrangement without waiting weeks for an information-technology change. The system supports the trial and the record; the sales procedure concerns what participants do with the discrepancy and what they learn from the next response. [SL-W015]

Paired customer exploration and the use of prototypes#

山中正樹’s exploratory study concerns the Japan business of a precision-instrument company of US origin. Its business-development group separates exploration responsibility from short-term order targets while bringing technical and sales participants together in customer work. The study interviewed two technical-sales staff, two sellers and one marketer between December 2016 and February 2017. This company is distinct from the industrial-parts wholesaler discussed above. [SL-W028]

Paired preparation develops hypothetical needs from market and technical knowledge, including understanding of the customer’s customer. During an encounter, one participant can speak while the other notices, supplements and interprets. Subsequent internal discussion develops a shared account. Yamanaka distinguishes credibility obtained by competent responses to explicit needs from the trust that supports deeper dialogue. Pairing and debriefing help connect what was noticed to what can be explored next. [SL-W028]

Minimum prototypes are then used by customers. Use can reveal needs that were not specified beforehand; the firm can revise the offering, and a customized product may develop into a standard one. Yamanaka’s interpretation connects exploration responsibility, complementary attention, discussion and actual customer use. The reported contrast with less successful solo work forms part of this exploratory case and the author’s proposed explanation. [SL-W028] The contrast includes technical staff working alone who discuss technology without establishing the same exchange; joint interpretation within the organization is part of the proposed account, as well as pairing during the visit. [SL-W028]
