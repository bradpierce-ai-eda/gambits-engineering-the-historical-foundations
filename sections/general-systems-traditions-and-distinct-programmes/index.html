A field of programmes, models and questions#

General systems writing contains several different enterprises: identifying recurrent mathematical relations, describing organization across levels, explaining maintenance and development, and arranging inquiry across disciplines.

The International Society for the Systems Sciences describes its founding purposes as identifying isomorphies across fields, encouraging useful model transfers, reducing duplicated theoretical effort and improving communication between specialists. These are substantive proposals for organizing intellectual work. Researchers might reuse an appropriate formal result instead of independently rebuilding it in each discipline. [SG-W006, main body]

Rapoport's account presents general systems theory as a programme or direction of inquiry rather than one particular scientific theory. Its integrating purpose concerns concepts and research methods and permits distinct domain theories. [SG-W037, opening]

Boulding's The Skeleton of Science proposes two routes. One starts with phenomena that recur across disciplines and asks whether their relations support common models. The other arranges systems by increasing organizational complexity: static arrangements, dynamic mechanisms, control systems, self-maintaining systems, and further biological, symbolic and social levels. The hierarchy is a research scaffold. A model that captures a lower level may leave out precisely what matters at a higher one. An organization cannot be adequately explained as a thermostat merely because both involve feedback. [SG-W036, article]

This warning is consequential for economy claims. A reduction in the variables of a model is useful only if the description still answers the question. Boulding's article seeks intermediate generality between isolated specialisms and contentless universality. Its historical judgments about biological and social levels are presented as parts of the 1956 programme, rather than current empirical conclusions. [SG-W036]

Bertalanffy: what formal recurrence can and cannot show#

The 1950 article An Outline of General System Theory develops a connected argument about interacting parts, differentiation, control, maintenance and finality. Bertalanffy's ambition is to find principles applicable to systems as systems, while preserving the special laws and problems of particular fields. [W072, printed 134–165]

He offers several reasons why similar mathematical forms recur. There are relatively restricted mathematical schemes available for solution; some system conditions themselves recur; and formally analogous relations can occur among physically different variables. These possibilities require discrimination. Writing two processes with the same equation does not show that their constituent things, causes or measurements are identical. A proposed mapping must preserve the relevant relations. Otherwise, apparent generality may come from a convenient notation or a superficial analogy. [W072, 137–142]

His treatment of aggregate variables supplies a further condition. Replacing a multitude of particulars with a smaller number of state quantities can make analysis possible, but the quantities must be adequate for the process under study. An aggregate description needs empirical and theoretical warrant. [W072, 141–146]

The finite system of differential equations illustrates interactions among changing quantities. The quadratic growth form is not necessarily sigmoid for arbitrary coefficients and initial conditions; the characteristic-root discussion contains a sign error; and the allometry discussion gives inconsistent forms for a constant and its inequality. [W072, 145–146, 151–152]

Interaction, specialization and the loss of restoration#

Wholeness in the article means that the behavior of interacting components differs from what their isolated behavior would imply. Analysis of components remains useful, but it must be joined to an account of their relations. It says that removing a relation can change the phenomenon being explained, so that a parts inventory is insufficient. [W072, 146–150]

Progressive segregation and mechanization describe a change in organization. Components become more differentiated and their activities more specifically assigned. Specialization can support effective differentiated performance. At the same time, a component that once could participate in restoring several functions may lose that flexibility. Greater fixed assignment can make replacement or regulation of a damaged whole more difficult. The article thus discusses a gain accompanied by a possible loss. [W072, 147–150]

A differentiated whole may perform a task well because its parts no longer have to reproduce every function. That arrangement can also depend more heavily on particular parts continuing to work. Bertalanffy's examples connect organization with specialization and restoration. The functions and disturbances being compared must remain explicit. [W072, 147–150]

His discussion of a leading part also separates control from the energy of performance. A small change can initiate or direct a much larger response because the responding process draws energy from elsewhere. The control signal need not supply all the energy released. This gives a concrete meaning to an energetically small determining influence. His nested levels similarly concern organization; a hierarchy in this sense need not be a hierarchy of human authority. [W072, 150–151]

Open maintenance and conditional equifinality#

Bertalanffy's open-system account concerns maintenance through material exchange. A living organization can retain an approximately stable pattern while its constituents are continually replaced. Its persistence is an ongoing process involving import, transformation and export. A steady state is therefore different from an unchanging collection of material pieces. In this discussion, “closed” refers to absence of material exchange; it should not silently be strengthened to complete energetic isolation. [W072, 155–158]

Equifinality names arrival at a similar final condition from different initial conditions or through different routes. The important mechanism is the system's organization and continuing processes under appropriate conditions. The endpoint need not encode a unique history. This helps explain why observing the maintained condition does not by itself reveal the complete starting arrangement. [W072, 157–159]

The article's biological qualifications matter: impaired conditions can prevent restoration, and the relevant rates and resources remain necessary. More generally, the claim needs a stable regime, an appropriate basin and maintained parameters. Openness alone does not exclude multiple stable outcomes. Nor is every materially closed system incapable of convergence under its constraints. The account retained here is conditional equifinality, not the blanket opposition between inevitable closed-system history dependence and inevitable open-system convergence. [W072, 157–159]

The account of entropy likewise retains exchange across the boundary. A local increase in organization is not a violation of the second law when the surrounding flows are included. The article extends minimum-

entropy-production reasoning beyond its applicable regimes. Its stellar illustration also involves exchanges with the surroundings. [W072, 161–162]

Different senses of finality#

Bertalanffy distinguishes several phenomena often grouped under purpose. Static fitness concerns an arrangement suited to a function. Dynamic finality concerns movement toward a condition. A machine can have structure that guides its operation; feedback can correct departures. An open organization can maintain or recover a state through continuing exchange. Human foresight and purposive language add a different issue. These descriptions should not all become claims that the future causes the present. [W072, 154–155, 159–160]

This distinction preserves significant information for gambits engineering. A suitable arrangement, a correcting loop and an anticipating actor may each affect how performance is achieved. Its final discussion leaves ultimate reduction of the sciences open and rejects treating domain-specific inquiry as dispensable. Generality is pursued alongside the autonomy of particular problems. [W072, 164–165]

Bateson: restraints and levels of choice#

Bateson's 1967 Cybernetic Explanation, explains events by examining alternatives that restraints exclude. A jigsaw piece is constrained by neighboring shapes, the boundary and the picture. The same features act as clues for a solver and restraints in an explanation of what can fit. The account shifts attention from an isolated object to the relations that make some possibilities more likely than others. [SG-W009, PDF 2–3]

His discussion of mapping concerns relations preserved through transformations. A formal model or simulation can be informative when its relevant correspondences hold. It is not enough to substitute one vocabulary for another. His analogy with logical proof describes the form of cybernetic explanation. [SG-W009, PDF 3–4]

Energy is explicitly retained. In a communication sequence, the response commonly uses the respondent's own energy: a neuronal response or an activated circuit need not be powered entirely by the triggering signal. The process still obeys conservation, and supply can limit what happens. A telephone network can also run out of available routes before exhausting its energy supply; alternative pathways are a different limiting dimension. [SG-W009, PDF 5]

Feedback constrains what happens after a disturbance has traveled around a circuit. The effect depends on gain, timing, thresholds and other characteristics. A circuit is causally closed in the sense of returning influence, while remaining open to energy and outside events. Thus the word “closed” here identifies a causal path, rather than the material-exchange boundary used in Bertalanffy's discussion. Keeping those meanings explicit prevents an apparent contradiction created solely by terminology. [SG-W009, PDF 5–6]

The distinction between levels of choice is especially useful. Once a person chooses a familiar word, its spelling can be available as a pattern; each letter need not be independently selected by eliminating every wrong letter. A higher-level choice can remove lower-level choices from the immediate task. The explanation retains the already established pattern. [SG-W009, PDF 6–7]

Redundancy also depends on the observer's prior knowledge. A person who knows a pattern can infer something about one portion from another. Someone who first has to discover the pattern faces a different task, so the apparent location and availability of information change with the explanatory boundary. Bateson's discussion of educated and initially uninformed observers makes that dependence concrete. [SG-W009, PDF 8–10]

Foerster: the boundary of self-organization#

In the selected passages of On Self-Organizing Systems and Their Environments, Foerster challenges an account that attributes increased order to an isolated system while neglecting its environment. What counts as self-organization depends on the chosen boundary and on the resources exchanged across it. His opening provocation should be read with that argument, rather than as a literal denial of every process called self-organization. [W080, 2003 reprint 1–3, 6]

The magnetic-cube illustration is concrete. The components have selective properties built into them, and the surrounding process supplies opportunities for interaction. The resulting arrangement is not explained by undifferentiated matter spontaneously acquiring every relevant discrimination. Component design and environmental conditions belong in the explanation. [W080, 11–13, discussion 19]

Maruyama: amplification can generate a history#

Maruyama's 1963 The Second Cybernetics distinguishes deviation-counteracting from deviation-amplifying mutual causal processes. Both involve reciprocal influence. Counteraction can preserve a pattern; amplification can build differences and produce new patterns. “Second” here names a different kind of process, not automatically the later programme of observer-centered second-order cybernetics. [SG-W061, PDF 1–2]

His economic discussion attributes to Myrdal a circular account of cumulative advantage and disadvantage. Poor health, low working efficiency, low income and exclusion can reinforce one another. Institutions such as transport, education and insurance can affect the direction of the process. [SG-W061, PDF 2–3]

The city illustration separates the initial event from subsequent development. On an initially homogeneous plain, a farm may attract other farms, a tool shop and further services. Increased activity supports more activity. The eventual location need not have had a decisive prior geographical advantage. A historian who seeks only a large initial cause may overlook the cumulative process. The story is an explanatory illustration. [SG-W061, PDF 3–4]

Small initial differences can have large consequences even in deterministic processes. Difficult inverse reconstruction does not imply absence of forward causation. Maruyama's development per initial investment differs from efficiency per total resource input; amplification does not overturn the second law. [SG-W061, PDF 3–7]

The developmental models make the account more specific. Local rules and ordered phases can generate elaborate patterns without storing a separate instruction for every eventual cell. Their operation nevertheless retains a rule set, an interpreting process, an initial state, spatial relations, occupancy and time-dependent stages. [SG-W061, PDF 8–11]

Maruyama also warns that apparent bilateral traffic need not establish mutual causal dependence. A producer may have alternative suppliers or customers; a third activity may drive both sides. An account of dependence needs the substitution possibilities and boundary, rather than arrows showing transactions alone. This qualification is directly relevant to identifying which relations actually determine an organization's performance. [SG-W061, PDF 11–12]

Loop signs describe directional effects, not moral values. An amplifying process can be destructive, while a stable pattern can preserve a harmful condition. Negative feedback can oscillate under particular delays and gains; mixed networks depend on strengths and operating regimes as well as signs. The proposed inventing machine combines search, amplification and counteraction, but is a proposal rather than a demonstrated result. [SG-W061, PDF 13–16]

The 1988 author retrospective is a separate source. Maruyama reports that the popular article omitted mathematics from earlier work, describes repeated rejection, and explains that “second” meant a different kind rather than a serial stage. He gives heterogeneity an increasingly explicit positive role. These are valuable statements of the author's interpretation and intellectual development. [RGC-W037, retrospective]

Qian's knowledge architecture and its later reconstruction#

The selected reprint of Qian Xuesen's 1986 speech treats departments of science as perspectives on a connected whole and distinguishes basic science, technical science and engineering, with philosophical relations across the arrangement. This is an account of how inquiry and application can be organized. [WME-W027, 2021 reprint, discussion of the organization of science and technology]

The institutional historical article credited to the Chinese Academy of Sciences reconstructs an expansion from “1+3” to “1+11” fields and emphasizes their connections. Its relevance is the changing architecture through which disciplines and practical work were related. [WME-W028, 2010 main body]

Lu Mingsen’s 2004 reconstruction and Yu Jingyuan’s 2001 account explain the open-complex-giant-systems programme developed in the 1990 Qian–Yu–Dai article and Yu’s 1992 work. [W035; W036; RGC-W039, 17–23; RGC-W040, 10–18]

Lu describes a movement from systems engineering toward problems whose scale, heterogeneity and human participation make a purely decompositional treatment inadequate. The proposed response integrates experts, data, computing, and qualitative and quantitative inquiry. Seminars, broad expertise and organized collective work remain part of the process. [RGC-W039, article]

Metasynthesis retains people, machines and revision#

Yu's 2001 account makes the method operationally clearer. Scientific theory, experience and expert judgment first contribute provisional hypotheses, plans or conjectures. Machines and networks handle suitable logical, numerical, storage and communication tasks. Human-led comparison and successive revision connect qualitative judgments with quantitative work and micro descriptions with macro questions. The method is intended to increase the capacity of inquiry through a deliberately composed human–machine arrangement. [RGC-W040, printed 16–17]

His expert, machine and knowledge systems are mutually connected. Experts and machines carry knowledge; they do not make it unnecessary. Collection, storage, retrieval, transmission, analysis and the production of further knowledge remain activities of the arrangement. Decomposition is still used, followed by integration. The programme's opposition to an inadequate reductionism therefore should not be rewritten as a prohibition on analyzing parts. [RGC-W040, 17]

The symbolic “1+1>2” expresses an aspiration to emergent advantage. It is not a measured inequality in one defined unit of knowledge, cost or performance. Yu's claim that repeated comparison yields a scientific conclusion describes the intended process; iteration by itself cannot guarantee correctness without adequate evidence. His division of capabilities between people and computers is a 2001 position, not a proved permanent limit on computation. [RGC-W040, 16–17]

The 2007 Research on Meta-synthetic Methodology and Systemics preview provides important self-qualification. Its printed opening asks about necessary and sufficient conditions for “1+1>2” as an unresolved theoretical problem. The programme is described as embryonic. [W078, preview 8–12]

The authors describe practical difficulties in a project involving multiple institutions. A central group could not simply command all participants, and software working inside individual groups did not

automatically interoperate. Funders, researchers seeking innovation and practical users brought different requirements. Even the same experts could reach different conclusions under a different convener or purpose. Knowledge integration consequently includes coordination of institutions, interfaces and purposes, not merely aggregation of statements. [W078, preview 4–10 and prefatory material]

The preview's iterative procedure retains changing goals, thresholds, feedback and limited relative optimization. Its network recommendations require substitutable capabilities and surviving connections; decentralization alone does not prove robustness. Its proposed multi-agent arrangements still include membership management, interaction mechanisms and standards. Security has managerial and social aspects, while an apparently friendly interface can require substantial development and knowledge of users. [W078, preview 9–12]

WSR: physical conditions, organized work and human acceptance#

The opening of Gu and Tang's 2002 WSR chapter introduces 物理—事理—人理, commonly rendered Wuli –Shili–Renli. The orientation combines attention to physical and technical conditions, the organization of activities, and human understanding and interests. The authors map historical influences across cultures. The 1997 article below develops a practical application. [WME-W026, printed 16–21; RGC-W041]

Gu and colleagues' article on a commercial-facilities standards framework makes the three aspects concrete. Technical standards must respect actual equipment, material and economic capabilities. They should also support improvement rather than freeze poor performance. Organizing the standards into a coherent framework is another problem: standards need suitable levels and relations to other systems. Human acceptance is equally operative because experts, managers and users must understand, negotiate and implement the result. [RGC-W041, printed 134–137]

The reported workflow begins with literature and expert inquiry, pools results within a multidisciplinary project group, extends inquiry through a wider survey, and returns to group and expert consideration with computer analysis and display. Brainstorming generates alternatives; Delphi treatment and clustering help interpret opinions, including differences associated with respondents' backgrounds. The editable computer knowledge base preserves the framework and survey results for later retrieval, revision and supplementation. Reusability is achieved through an organized maintenance process. [RGC-W041, 135–136, fig. 1]

The reported compromise combines a three-and-a-half-level structure, broad inclusion of related standards and selective detail at the lowest level. The resulting framework contains eight systems, sixty-two specialties, forty related standards and sixty-seven entries. The authors report official appraisal in 1996. [RGC-W041, 136–137]

The article's CATWOE presentation also requires care: it announces six elements but initially lists five, omitting the ownership element subsequently used for leadership opinion; its transformation description is narrower than the general term. [RGC-W041, 135–137]

Other accounts of relations, identity and integration#

Dostal's There are Systems and Systems, in its 2025 revision of the 2023 proceedings paper, connects entities through activities and treats entities as emerging from interwoven activities. Aims, processes, structures, governance and environmental flows belong in the account. “Tapping” makes an output's actual reception and use a condition of an effective interface: producing something does not ensure that another activity can employ it. Opposed sequences of design and persistence describe the encounter between intended change and existing organization. [SG-W004, 5–14]

Dostal’s conceptual synthesis advances practical hypotheses about organizational arrangements. Its information-field and healing ontology extends the argument into speculative claims beyond the organizational examples. [SG-W004, 16–19]

The selected Schwarz excerpt connects action procedures to models and then to underlying beliefs and values. A procedure's practical success, a model's validity and a claim about truth are related questions with different warrants. The relevance is the dependence of organized action on conceptual and evaluative commitments. [SG-W013]

The two Principia Cybernetica pages on metasystem transition describe copies or variants brought into an arrangement with an additional control mechanism. Mere aggregation is insufficient. Control over an activity becomes an activity at another level, and the controller need not be spatially detached from what it controls. Its broader evolutionary progression is a theoretical proposal. [SG-W032, both pages]

Bennett's Systematics and System Theories treats a system through qualitatively differentiated terms and their mutual relevance. Compresence and the functional identity of the whole matter alongside pairwise interaction. Successive orders of terms provide ways to organize qualitative judgment. This is a vocabulary for examining kinds of relatedness. [SG-W033, body]

Moreira's reconstruction of Bunge supplies a different explanatory demand through composition, environment, structure and mechanism. A list of components and their bonds can leave novelty unexplained unless the processes that produce and sustain it are specified. The company illustration places activities and environmental interaction inside the description. [SG-W043, composition, environment, structure and mechanism]

Volk and Bloom's metapatterns article proposes a provisional vocabulary of recurrent forms and functions. Borders combine separation with regulated exchange; tubes, webs and centers organize different relations; holons and clonons, triggers and cycles identify further recurring possibilities. Convergence under similar conditions is part of the rationale, with differences acknowledged between cultural and genetic processes. The extension to educational templates is an assumption the authors say has not been rigorously tested. [SG-W044, PDF 1–11, 13 opening]

Wu's letter to pansystems friends links generalized relations, observation and control, parts and wholes, transformation and optimization. Its unusually pertinent qualification is that generalized language should not become an all-encompassing slogan: useful application still requires knowledge of the field in question. [SG-W049, letter, part 1]

Maps and organizational qualifications#

Zhang Hao’s 2022 Management Science Research Models and Methods, second edition, distinguishes the “old” and “new” triads and sets out multiple schools, including mathematical systems work, operations research, dynamics, complex evolution, dissipative structures, synergetics, catastrophe approaches and systems management. It also names grey systems, pansystems and matter-element analysis. [SG-W005, preview 3–13]

The University of Shanghai for Science and Technology's 2021 account similarly names hypercycles, living and social systems, pansystems and grey systems among the branches of systems science. Its principles supply an institutional teaching presentation of the field. [SG-W007]

Kast and Rosenzweig's selected organizational passages bring the issue back to explicit boundaries and contingencies. Organizational survival, consequences for society and outcomes for participants are different criteria. A configuration useful under one set of conditions need not suit another, and a contrived organization should not be identified exactly with a biological organism. The account calls for

specifying levels, relevant relations and conditions rather than relying on systems language alone. [SG-W056, printed 447–448, 455–457]
