How a surface flaw enters the fracture question#

A. A. Griffith introduces his 1921 paper through a question about filing, grinding and polishing machine parts subjected to repeated loads. He examines stresses near scratches and develops an energetic account of extending a flaw. The argument connects a change in the crack with changes in the energy of the stressed body and the cost of creating new surfaces. His controlled specimens make aspects of that relation observable. They are distinct from the machine parts that motivated the inquiry. [T12-W001]

Properties of a particular processed material#

Chen, Zhang and Ritchie tested a silicon carbide ceramic whose grain boundaries were initially glassy. In the as-processed material, measured fracture toughness was 6.2 MPa√m at 25 °C and 4.3 MPa√m at 1300 °C. After annealing for 85 hours at 1300 °C, the corresponding values were 7.2 and 5.2 MPa√m. Microscopy showed that most grain-boundary films had crystallized. In the authors’ account of fatigue, cracks extend between grains while intact grains bridging the crack behind its tip oppose its advance. The processing comparison comes from fracture-toughness measurements. The crack-growth observations come from the cyclic tests. [T12-W002]

Service conditions and specimens#

中村眞実 examines stainless-steel weld metal in a high-pressure hydrogen setting. The dissertation describes a pre-cooler used in a 70 MPa demonstration station and gives a 77 MPa condition for hydrogen in the cooling pipe. Its earlier discussion treats other hydrogen charging and tensile tests with specified weld metals and treatments. Fracture surfaces and reduction of area supply observations under those conditions. The component establishes an operating context, while a test specimen provides a particular material, exposure and loading arrangement. [T12-W003]

Motooka and Kiuchi compare load-controlled fatigue crack growth for refractory materials in boiling nitric acid and in air at room temperature. The stress-intensity range provides the axis of comparison. They motivate the study through reprocessing equipment. The two test settings differ in both environment and temperature, so the comparison describes behavior under two combined sets of conditions. [T12-W004]
