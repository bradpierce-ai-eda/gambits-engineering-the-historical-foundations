Distinct questions about organized persistence#

The sources in this section pursue different projects. Iberall and Soodak propose a physical account of nested complex dynamics. Kelso develops coordination dynamics with empirically identified variables and task-dependent meaning. Gell-Mann distinguishes kinds of complexity. An abridgment of Laszlo’s systems philosophy and reconstructions by Huchingson and Leaning introduce a wider ontological proposal.

Homeokinetics: long actions within nested dynamics#

The Primer on Homeokinetics describes complex systems through a separation of time scales: a long internal action or “factory day” differs from relatively rapid local exchanges among components. Horizontal interactions and vertical relations among nested levels are both needed in the proposed account. Describing a system requires identifying those physical processes at an appropriate observational scale. [WME-W012, printed 2–8]

The authors distinguish a mathematical framework from the physical and engineering path that connects it with an actual system. Discrete descriptions and continuum approximations also have different conditions. Their declared physical reductionism does not mean simply announcing that biology reduces to chemistry and chemistry to physics; the work lies in specifying the intervening organization, exchanges and scales. The primer supplies a programme and examples. [WME-W012, 2–10]

The supplier analogy makes one relevant claim concrete. A factory could not operate within a compact time and place if every subcomponent at every level had to be constructed anew. Previously produced components arrive from other processes. Local operation employs a history and a surrounding organization of supply. The example explains why the time and capability represented by a supplied component cannot be attributed solely to the current act inside the factory. [WME-W012, 8]

Formation, a long maintenance phase, and degradation are separated. The action sustaining a familiar structure may differ from what assembled it or what eventually dissolves it. Low-energy catalytic changes can switch among more energetic action modes. In that account, a signal negotiates a transition while the active process retains the energy and material needed for performance. A small switch is not a source of the whole activity's energy. [WME-W012, 10–15]

The grandfather clock is an especially concrete example. A falling weight supplies gravitational energy, the escapement couples to pendular motion, gearing produces further time scales, and periodic winding replenishes the store. Oiling and a suitable suspension remain necessary. The authors examine transient changes after winding as well as sustained timekeeping. [WME-W012, 11–15]

The oil-can oscillator and other illustrations similarly involve specific couplings and replenishment. The primer is interested in how relatively autonomous motions become coordinated while retaining different scales. The proposed physical explanation requires the forces, energy source and stability conditions. [WME-W012, 11–16]

Its house illustration extends the maintenance distinction accessibly. A house may look like a completed solid object, but repair and replacement require continuing flows of materials, costs and action from its first use. The maintenance engineer sees a persistent flow process where an owner may initially see a finished construction. [WME-W012, 21]

The proposed social physics remains unfinished#

The social passages extend physical language to population, trade, political organization and war. The authors intend condensed and dispersed population phases as literal physical explanations, not merely decorative analogies, and propose civilizational time scales. That intention does not establish the corresponding equations, measurements or historical validity. [WME-W012, 16–23]

The primer's final qualification is unusually explicit. Its account of values and command-control is far from complete or adequately determined, and the hoped-for ultimate neurochemical reduction may be a false hope. [WME-W012, 23]

The abstract of Iberall’s 1985 Outlining Social Physics for Modern Societies proposes flows of matter, energy, momentum, population and value in trade. The later primer develops his nested physical architecture. [WME-W013, abstract; WME-W012]

Kelso: coordination with continuing autonomy#

Kelso's 2008 An Essay on Understanding the Mind engages sympathetically with Iberall but develops a distinct account. Coordination concerns how several processes form a collective pattern while retaining their own tendencies. Its functional information is meaningful in relation to a task and the activity organized, rather than a free-standing count of messages. Relevant order variables and control parameters must be identified empirically. [RGC-W017, 180–190]

The extended Haken–Kelso–Bunz relation includes a difference between the intrinsic frequencies of the participating oscillators, nonlinear coupling and noise. The relative phase can then exhibit regimes that the original symmetric equation did not contain. Kelso expressly warns against assigning the extension's results to the unchanged original equation: symmetry breaking changes fixed points and bifurcation structure, and is crucial to the metastable behavior being discussed. [RGC-W017, 188 and notes 2–3]

Fully locked coordination and complete independence are limiting cases. A metastable regime contains tendencies both to bind together and to act independently. Components can linger in a coordinated relation and then depart, rather than remaining permanently locked in one asymptotically stable pattern. The physical components and their coupling remain necessary. [RGC-W017, 187–193]

Kelso proposes dwell and escape times to describe these tendencies. Dwell time concerns the persistence of a collective tendency; escape time concerns expression of the elements' autonomy. Their ratio is a candidate description of the quality of metastability. The measures require operational definitions, and any transition threshold must be worked out for the particular system. [RGC-W017, 192–193, notes 5–6]

The coordination issue is therefore not solved by maximizing either synchronization or independence. Too little coupling can prevent a useful collective activity; too much locking can remove flexibility. Which relation is satisfactory depends on the task. The essay's comparison across behavioral and neural examples is a programme of identifiable relationships. [RGC-W017, 190–202]

Switching also need not require a localized physical switch. A distributed pattern can lose stability and another pattern become possible through the dynamics of interaction. The explanation retains oscillators, coupling, changing parameters and energy. Absence of one command location does not mean absence of determining processes or continuing work. [RGC-W017, 199–202]

The essay relates stability to reported neural measurements, including BOLD changes as coordinated patterns lose stability. The broader extensions to mind, social activity and meditation remain tentative essay proposals. [RGC-W017, 199, note 10, concluding discussion]

Gell-Mann: a short account can be hard to find and use#

What Is Complexity? distinguishes information measures from time and space required for computation. Algorithmic information content concerns the shortest programme producing a specified string and halting. An incompressible random string can have high content by that definition, yet random gibberish does not capture every ordinary meaning of complexity. Gell-Mann therefore distinguishes a concise description of regularities from the complete specification of the individual entity. [WME-W014, 16–17]

His effective complexity concerns those regularities. The description depends on coarse-graining, prior knowledge, language, coding and the reference machine. Two apparently different measures can answer different questions. A long string containing a very simple repeated relation can have much mutual algorithmic information while its organizing regularity has a brief description. The amount of shared pattern and the length of the rule describing it are not identical. [WME-W014, 16–17]

The adaptive-schema account then connects observation and activity. A system identifies regularities and compresses them into a schema used for description, prediction or prescribed behavior. Comparison with further data and the consequences of action affect competition among schemata. This process can make mistakes: a pattern can be imagined where it is absent, or an actual relation ignored. The account does not treat every acquired rule as correct merely because it is compact. [WME-W014, 17–18]

Prediction and discovery add separate demands. A simple rule with specified boundary conditions can require lengthy computation to produce a detailed prediction. Finding a short rule from data can be difficult even when using it later is straightforward. Gell-Mann relates these questions to logical depth and crypticity, while saying that a more precise mathematical account will appear elsewhere. [WME-W014, 18–19]

Gell-Mann separates a description from the work of discovering it and computing its predictions. Compactness of a rule can coexist with difficult discovery or lengthy execution. [WME-W014]

Laszlo's patterned flows and a mediated larger architecture#

The anonymous abridgment of Laszlo's The Systems View of the World presents enduring structures through patterned flows. A structure channels later activity, while that activity sustains or changes the organization. Human action belongs within wider ecological and social relations rather than an isolated world of self-sufficient individuals. [SG-W038]

Huchingson's article and Leaning's thesis reconstruct the foundational philosophy of Laszlo's Introduction to Systems Philosophy, distinct from the popular The Systems View of the World. [RGC-W018; RGC-W019; RGC-W020; RGC-W021]

Huchingson reconstructs a connected ontology of natural and cognitive systems: ordered wholeness, adaptive stabilization, self-organization and nested relations. Systems can contain other systems, and an enduring identity is formed through relations to an environment. Greater organization permits alternative programmes of action and can support modifying surroundings. The account is a philosophical architecture, not merely a list of examples. [RGC-W020, discussion of reality and environment]

He also reconstructs Laszlo's connection between adaptation, persistence, experience and value. Cognitive systems can pursue imagined conditions, while knowledge arises through observer–world interaction. These are proposed ontological and epistemological relations. Survival or satisfaction does not automatically settle a human ethical choice. [RGC-W020, environment, values and causality sections]

The reconstructions include methodological criticism#

Huchingson's comparison with Whitehead preserves differences. Whitehead's actual occasions are not simply Laszlo's nested composite systems. He asks whether an abstract grid is being imposed on experience, whether the primordial matrix coheres with the universal system categories, and how emergence addresses differences in agency. In particular, a matrix that generates systems while not itself being a system raises a problem for the ontology as presented. [RGC-W020]

The article's later theological reconciliation and information-processing extensions belong to Huchingson. [RGC-W020, critical comparison, conclusion and notes]

Leaning's selected section 3.4.3 reconstructs the same broad programme while questioning its grounds for validation. Presupposing that the world is systematically ordered does not establish that a particular general model adequately describes it. Combining correspondence-based confirmation with theory-laden scientific paradigms also creates methodological tensions that require explanation. The objection concerns justification and empirical productivity. [RGC-W021, §3.4.3, printed pp. 89–92/PDF pp. 92–95]
