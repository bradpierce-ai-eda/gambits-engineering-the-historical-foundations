From a chart signal to an investigation#

In his 1931 book, Walter Shewhart asks how fluctuations in the fraction defective might tell an investigator when to look for an assignable cause. Limits derived from previous observations give a basis for examining later points. In the example behind Fig. 6, he reports that the proportion of observations falling within the limits rose from 68 to 84 percent as causes were removed. The statistic counts points within the limits. Shewhart adds that even a point inside them can have an assignable cause. A chart supplies a signal for inquiry while the investigation establishes what changed. [T13-W001]

The NIST/SEMATECH handbook distinguishes the available specification width from the spread and centering of the process. A process can have room within the limits if centered yet put more pieces beyond one specification because its mean lies near that limit. The handbook treats capability estimates in the context of stable behavior and gives assumptions for converting them to expected reject fractions. Sampling uncertainty also affects the estimate. [T13-W002]

Designing for measurement and improvement#

仁科健 treats manufacturing as a recipient of the designer’s work. Design choices affect both ease of production and the ease with which an abnormal result can be detected. His exposition considers materials, equipment, operators, methods, measurement and surroundings. Finding a possible cause in the data leads to a further question about the technical relationship, for which he discusses experimental design. [T13-W003]

Sharma, Sahni and Sharma examine a measurement system whose burst test consumes the piece measured. Repeating a test under the same settings requires other pieces judged comparable, so variation within a batch enters the assessment of repeatability. The authors use a crossed gauge study as one part of evaluating the equipment and discuss examining unusual observations and repeating measurements under the same settings. Their case concerns the quality of the measurement system. [T13-W004]

Following a molding experiment into production#

สุรศักดิ์ ชุบไธสง and ระพี กาญจนะ studied silver streaking on injection-molded telephone part 1220402. They used a fractional factorial screen on eight candidate settings and then a central composite design to examine four factors. The combination they took forward used 97 MPa injection pressure, 240 tons clamping force, a nozzle temperature of 237 °C and injection speed of 30 mm/s. Screening, confirmation and later production supplied different observations. [T13-W005]

Their part-specific comparison records 1,092 affected pieces out of 32,357 before the change, 663 out of 42,878 during confirmation production and 723 out of 106,665 in later mass production. Those counts give about 33,748, 15,462 and 6,778 pieces with silver streaks per million. The observations concern one defect on one part across different production periods. The authors also call for monitoring other defect modes when applying the settings. [T13-W005]
