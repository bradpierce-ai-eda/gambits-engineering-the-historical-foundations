Arrangements that preserve a later choice#

The real-options passages and the scholarly reconstruction of Katsenelinboigen concern decisions whose consequences cannot all be specified in advance. An intermediate arrangement can preserve a future action, improve a position or avoid premature commitment.

Smit and Trigeorgis: cash flows, flexibility and strategic interaction#

The opening of Strategic Investment: Real Options and Games links corporate finance and strategic planning. Smit and Trigeorgis distinguish appraisal of projected cash flows from the additional value that managerial flexibility and strategic interaction can bring to growth opportunities. Their framework treats those opportunities as a package of real options actively managed by a firm, with competitors and new technologies capable of changing their value. It is therefore an account of contingent choices in a competitive setting, not simply a favorable valuation of uncertainty. [AFC-W017, chapter 1, selected opening, printed pp. 3–7]

Their diagram relates project appraisal, planning of growth opportunities and competitive strategy to an expanded valuation. Discounted cash-flow analysis contributes one part; real-option reasoning considers adaptive capability; game-theoretic industrial organization addresses the interaction with competitors. The point of linking the approaches is that a firm's action need not leave the strategic environment unchanged. Waiting, expanding or committing can affect, and be affected by, other actors.

The selected deferral passage uses partly irreversible capacity investment to explain why timing can matter. A plant that becomes unattractive after prices fall may not be saleable at its original cost. Exercising the investment opportunity destroys the possibility of waiting for further information before making that expenditure. In such a setting, a project just at a conventional zero-net-present-value cutoff need not be worth undertaking immediately: the authors describe a premium associated with giving up the option to defer. [AFC-W017, flexibility/deferral passage]

Their analogy to a call option separates the opportunity from its exercise. Holding the right to acquire an asset at a specified cost differs from already owning the asset. The passage also states a qualification: postponement to expiration in the analogy assumes no opportunity costs of waiting or dividend-like benefits of holding the asset. Those foregone benefits are not erased by calling waiting flexible. Similarly, the book's opening places competitors' moves in the problem.

An irreversible commitment changes the future choice set. Its value depends on cash flows, the exercise cost, information revealed over time and the opportunities lost by delay. The object is the firm's strategic investment valuation under specified circumstances.

Katsenelinboigen through a scholarly reconstruction#

The selected portions of the Filosofia encyclopedia entry reconstruct Katsenelinboigen's theory of predispositioning. The account distinguishes material, positional and relational parameters. Material resources form part of an arrangement; position concerns relations such as proximity or centrality; relational parameters in its biological and social examples include relations among entities. A predisposition is an intermediate arrangement whose connections are partly formed, rather than a complete program linking an initial state to a determined outcome. [WME-W016, selected definition and parameter sections]

Chess is the principal explanatory model in the account. A forced combination can be evaluated through a specified sequence toward an outcome, while positional play must assess a state without such a complete chain. Material can be sacrificed to improve a position whose later use remains uncertain. The relevant question is not only how many pieces remain, but what their arrangement allows.

The entry also distinguishes valuations tied to a particular initial position, goal, program and rules from more general valuations based primarily on the rules. Even the latter are not literally conditionless; the reconstruction explains their partial or qualified unconditionality. A broadly useful piece value and its value in a particular position answer different questions. The theory's strategic valuation is meant to support action when the eventual sequence cannot yet be specified. [WME-W016, selected valuation and style sections]

The account describes programming, predispositioning and randomness as different methods of relating stages of development. Programming supplies complete, consistent links; predispositioning prepares partly connected possibilities. The evaluation of an intermediate position is itself the difficult work. The entry's discussion of the calculus of predisposition emphasizes decomposing material and positional parameters and assigning weights; it also retains the role of experience and subjectivity when unique situations lack repeatable frequency data. [WME-W016, selected calculus and subjectivity sections]

A positional sacrifice is a judgment under uncertainty: it prepares possibilities whose eventual use cannot yet be specified. The resulting position can fail to produce the anticipated advantage.

Valuing a future choice and preparing a position#

Smit and Trigeorgis supply an attributed argument about valuation, irreversibility, timing and strategic interaction. The Katsenelinboigen reconstruction supplies an account of preparing and evaluating an intermediate position without a complete program. These accounts can help a reader locate questions about retained choice without turning real options and predisposition into synonyms.
