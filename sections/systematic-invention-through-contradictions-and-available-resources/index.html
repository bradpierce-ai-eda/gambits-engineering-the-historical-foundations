From an inventive situation to a conflict model#

Altshuller’s ARIZ-85-V begins by changing the description of a difficulty. An inventive situation can contain many inconveniences and possible ambitions. The mini-problem asks for a useful effect to be obtained, or a harmful effect removed, while retaining or simplifying the existing system. This formulation directs attention away from immediately adding a device and toward what, precisely, the system must do differently. The mature 1985 procedure develops the earlier inventive-problem tradition discussed in S44. [NC-W001]

The next task is to identify a conflicting pair and state two opposed conditions of an element. Improving one desired result under one condition can worsen another. The system’s principal function determines which condition to retain as the starting point. Intensifying that condition makes the conflict more explicit. The X-element names the change required to resolve it; the name does not commit the solver to another component. A change in a substance’s state can fulfil the requirement. [NC-W001]

In the radio-telescope example, conducting rods provide lightning protection but disturb reception. Reducing their presence helps reception while weakening protection. Because reception is the principal function, the worked analysis retains the condition that avoids interference and intensifies it to the absence of the conductor. The question becomes how protection can be obtained where the interfering rod is no longer present. [NC-W001]

This is a provisional model to be checked against the original situation. The procedure repeatedly returns the solver to the initial requirements rather than treating the first formulation as final. Choosing a conflict and intensifying it supplies a more precise problem; it has not yet supplied a physical means of solving it. [NC-W001]

The place and time of the contradiction#

The operating zone locates the conflict, and operating time distinguishes when the opposed requirements must hold. In the telescope analysis, the zone is the air column where a lightning conductor would have been. Air is already present and becomes a candidate resource. The ideal-result direction asks for protection without the obstruction that had accompanied it. Altshuller uses ideality to direct the search toward the required effect with less added complication. [NC-W001]

The opposed requirements can now be stated at a different scale. The column should conduct when a lightning discharge develops and remain nonconducting during ordinary reception. These conditions need not apply at the same time. At the particle level, free charges are wanted during discharge and neutral molecules otherwise. The reformulation separates a general demand for a transparent protector into a particular place, occasion and material behaviour. [NC-W001]

A resource inventory considers substances and fields in and around the system, including their states and possible changes. The fact that air has been identified does not yet explain how it will acquire the required charges. The next part of the worked analysis must make the behaviour physically attainable. Altshuller’s ideal-result formulation is a direction of inquiry, not a claim that an ideal answer is always obtainable; the program also permits outside substances or fields when existing resources are insufficient. [NC-W001]

Turning a physical requirement into a design#

The little-people representation develops the particle requirement by asking what must differ between the selected air column and the surrounding air. In technical terms, the selected gas must ionize more readily. Combining air with emptiness suggests reduced pressure. That change makes a resource already implicated in the problem behave differently under the developing field. [NC-W001]

The technical scheme then places gas in a sealed dielectric tube with pressure selected for discharge as the lightning field develops. Altshuller cites author’s certificate 177497 in connection with this answer. The ending of the example is therefore more specific than a proposal to use an ionized column: it includes the pressure change and the construction that maintains it. The source presents a worked design and cited invention. [NC-W001]

Later stages distinguish a physical answer from its technical realization. A composite problem may need to be divided, the selected conflict reconsidered, or the system placed within a larger one. Checks ask what contradiction has actually been removed, how the result can be controlled, and whether it works over repeated cycles. An unsatisfactory answer sends the solver back to the initial mini-problem. Other uses and an examination of the route to the answer follow. [NC-W001]

The thermal-protective suit is a separate example of moving to a supersystem. Liquid oxygen removes heat while evaporating and warming; it subsequently supplies respiration. Cooling and breathing become connected functions within a larger arrangement. This example changes the boundary of the technical problem, whereas the telescope example develops the material behaviour required within a selected zone. [NC-W001]

Standards, physical effects and other inventive principles form part of the program’s wider repertoire. The worked analysis shows why choosing among tools is embedded in reformulation, development and checking. A diagram of successive stages would miss the returns and changes of direction that the instructions make conditional on the emerging answer. [NC-W001]

Making a new dependency visible#

Goldenberg and Mazursky’s Creativity in Product Innovation organizes invention through representations of products and their relations. Their attribute-dependency procedure distinguishes components from variables. Connecting a chair leg to a backrest concerns components; making the length of one vary with the length of the other concerns a dependency between attributes. The latter representation can expose a relation that a component list alone would not show. [NC-W002]

Their forecasting matrix places manufacturer-controlled internal variables in columns, with internal and external variables in rows. Existing dependencies are marked. Selected empty cells then become possible changes: what would follow if these variables were made dependent? The direction of a proposed dependency matters because it specifies what is to change with what. [NC-W002]

A candidate relation is developed into a feasible construction and examined for a useful consequence, market benefit or new segment. The representation supplies possibilities that still require technical and commercial interpretation. It is a way of generating a different product concept, rather than simply ranking completed concepts already supplied by an inventor. [NC-W002]

Their ice-coated antenna-pole example has a different conflict from Altshuller’s lightning problem. A transportable pole is light enough to carry but insufficiently strong under ice loading. Installed freezing conditions allow strength to depend on a resource that is absent during transport: a roughened pole collects an ice coating while the antenna acquires its load. The source of the difficulty becomes part of the

proposed support. Lightness in transport and strength in service are separated by circumstances and time. [NC-W002]

Reassigning a function to what is already there#

Replacement temporarily removes a component from a product or process representation while retaining its function. The unassigned function prompts a search among components already available. Goldenberg and Mazursky illustrate the procedure with rejected butter patties that had been melted slowly in a steam-jacketed vat. Steam provides heat indirectly through the vessel. [NC-W002]

Removing the steam component leaves heating as a function that must still be supplied. The search is constrained by what else is present and by the properties needed for the task. Hot milk already used upstream can carry the function: it contacts and melts the butter directly. The resulting mixture can return to the production process. [NC-W002]

The intermediate steps explain the change. The inventors do not merely substitute the word milk for steam; they change the heat-transfer relation and the role of an existing process material. The butter and vessel remain intelligible parts of the revised arrangement, while a separate steam system is removed. [NC-W002]

The authors report faster melting and reduced time and energy costs. The case’s contribution is the connected reassignment from a removed component to an available one, followed through to the changed process. [NC-W002]

Six informational stages in USIT#

中川徹’s six-box USIT account distinguishes information produced during problem solving from a flowchart of named techniques. Its boxes contain a user’s concrete problem, an appropriately defined problem, understanding of the present and ideal systems, ideas for a new system, a solution concept, and the user’s concrete solution. The difference between an idea fragment and a technically coherent concept is explicit. [NC-W003]

Analysis develops objects, attributes and functions alongside relations in space and time. The present system is examined in relation to desired behaviour and properties. Operators then help generate nuclei of change. Technical knowledge is needed to combine those fragments into a concept that can answer the problem, rather than leaving the process at a collection of suggestions. [NC-W003]

Problem definition and concept construction form interfaces between practical work and the thinking process. Nakagawa identifies the conceptual solution as USIT’s output; concrete realization belongs to the user’s practical activity. He credits Sickafus’s earlier development and explains his own reorganization of TRIZ operators. His argument for a route that does not depend on first finding an external analogy concerns analysis of one’s own problem and construction from its results. [NC-W003]

What application and training studies report#

Goldenberg and Mazursky’s Study 3 compared methods with 120 participants divided among six method-by-product groups, using baby ointment or mattresses. Template instruction lasted about four hours, compared with about two hours for lateral thinking or random stimulation. A thirty-minute task produced 277 ideas, which three senior marketers rated blind for originality and investment value. Template ideas received higher ratings, including comparisons of selected leading ideas. Training time was therefore one difference in this design alongside the methods. [NC-W002]

Study 4 changed that comparison. Eighteen template trainees, nineteen HIT trainees and eighteen untrained participants generated drinking-glass ideas. Both trained groups received four hours of instruction. A different panel of three marketers rated the 82 ideas, again favouring the template condition. The two studies concern originality and value judgments of generated ideas, with the second addressing the earlier training-time difference. [NC-W002]

Birdi’s review chapter treats creativity training as a family of interventions whose tasks, delivery and setting matter. Systematic cognitive instruction, realistic practice, and support for evaluation as well as generation recur in his account. In his later description of the TRIZ workplace evaluation he coauthored, trainees showed stronger subsequent idea generation, while support for implementation change was more limited and included self-reported impact. The chapter distinguishes developing ideas from securing their use in an organization. [NC-W004]

Dumas, Schmidt and Alexander’s study of 44 engineering graduate students separates fluency from originality before and after TRIZ instruction. The reported post-instruction pattern contains fewer ideas but greater originality. Relational reasoning is the strongest reported predictor of originality, with antinomous reasoning implicated in the TRIZ-supported work. Their distinction among similarity, anomaly, incompatibility and opposition gives several kinds of relation a place in understanding the task. [NC-W005]

Spreafico and Russo classify more than two hundred published industrial applications by purpose, design activity, tools, combinations and output maturity. Contradictions and inventive principles predominate among reported tools, with QFD, FMEA and patent tools among their partners. The survey describes how tools are selected and combined in published engineering cases rather than treating TRIZ as a single intervention used identically everywhere. [NC-W006]

Their output distinctions range from a physical-effect hint to a concept with behaviour and structure, a detailed design and a market-ready product. Fewer than a quarter of the surveyed cases are classified as market-ready. The classification identifies what the reported application produced: a promising principle and a finished product are different endpoints, both of which may be useful in an engineering history. [NC-W006]

姚威、储昭卫 and 胡顺顺 examine 148 usable project reports from 216 collected in a supported innovation-method program. Two experienced researchers code tool uses and concepts, while expert ratings provide separate benefit measures. Productivity, quality and reliability are prominent aims in these reports of engineering problems. The method makes actual uses and the form of the resulting proposals available for comparison. [NC-W007]

Generating a concept and adopting it as a solution are counted separately. Contradiction and principle use is common, with a lower concepts-per-adopted-measure ratio than some other tools in the reports. This describes the relation between concepts and adopted measures in the selected field material. Alongside the published-case survey and the training studies, it gives a distinct view of how a proposed method enters engineering work. [NC-W007]
