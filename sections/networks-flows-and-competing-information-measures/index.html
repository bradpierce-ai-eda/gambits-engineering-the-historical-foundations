The sources examine support, connectivity and statistical dependence through accounts of maintained capacities, linked budgets, alternative paths and predictive relations.

MuSIASEM: maintained funds and linked flow budgets#

Giampietro, Mayumi and Ramos-Martin distinguish funds, whose identity is maintained during the accounting interval, from flows transformed during that interval. People, capital and land provide different kinds of fund; their continued availability requires maintenance and renewal. A rate of energy use per hour of human activity is consequently a relation between a flow and a fund, rather than an account of energy alone. Their account explains how descriptions at household, production and whole-society levels can constrain one another. What is feasible at one scale cannot be chosen independently of the other accounts. [SG-W017]

The authors call this a “Sudoku effect”: linked totals and component allocations restrict the combinations that can consistently be entered elsewhere. Human time allocated to production is unavailable for other activities, while production must support the activities and maintained funds outside its immediate boundary. A locally attractive intensity or productivity figure can therefore conceal an allocation that cannot reproduce the larger system. This is a consistency argument, not a claim that the equations select one uniquely desirable future. The distinction matters when an apparent saving in one production compartment depends on maintenance or supporting work located in another. [SG-W017, accepted manuscript §§1–2]

Categories are chosen before calculation. The narrative about what a system is and does determines which funds, flows and levels enter the representation. The authors expressly allow this narrative to fail: a fund may be unable to handle its proposed flow, cannot be maintained throughout the interval, or requires flows inconsistent with boundary conditions. They treat such failures as reasons to revise the representation. A fixed taxonomy is especially problematic for a system becoming something different, rather than merely changing its quantities within unchanged categories. [SG-W017]

The paper extends the account toward ecological constraints through land-use funds and densities of energy and material flows. The paper distinguishes areas whose flows are controlled by human activity from areas represented through ecosystem self-organization, while acknowledging that it cannot fully develop that application there. The model constrains linked budgets. [SG-W017]

Emergy: proposed accounting for paid and unpaid support#

Campbell, Brandt-Williams and Meisch’s West Virginia report proposes accounting for support that market payments can leave invisible. Its selected methodological pages define emergy as energy of one kind previously used, directly and indirectly, to produce a product or service. Transformity supplies a conversion factor between that accumulated supporting energy and a resulting quantity. The intended account includes ecological inputs as well as purchased inputs, so the production boundary extends beyond the immediate enterprise and its monetary accounts. [SG-W018, preface; §§1–2.2]

The proposed common basis depends on the specified transformations, system boundary, input data and treatment of co-products. An accounting convention does not, merely by assigning comparable numbers, demonstrate an equivalence among energy, knowledge and economic value. Allocation of shared inputs determines how co-products enter the supporting-energy account. The report itself calls its state account a first step because important subsystems remain outside the analysis. The report was published in 2005. [SG-W018]

Q-analysis: connectivity at different dimensions#

Smirnov, Kurkin and Hramov describe a Python package for Q-analysis and applications including network physiology. In the definitions, a simplex represents a set of incident elements. Two simplices are q-near when they share a q-dimensional face; q-connectivity extends the relation through a chain of such adjacencies. Increasing q makes the required overlap more demanding. The first structure vector records the number of q-connected components at successive dimensions. Connectivity is therefore a graded property of the chosen complex, rather than a single yes-or-no attribute of a graph. [SG-W022, selected definitions and discussion]

This representation can distinguish arrangements that simpler summaries obscure. A large complex connected through single shared vertices can fragment when the analysis requires shared edges or higher-dimensional faces. But the choice of incidence data and the construction of simplices determine what those differences describe. A clique extracted from pairwise observations does not itself demonstrate a causal interaction that requires the entire group. The authors characterize the core framework as exploratory and descriptive, useful for comparison and hypothesis generation. [SG-W022]

Reconstructability analysis: a simpler predictive distribution#

Shervais, Kramer, Westaway, Cox and Zwick explain reconstructability analysis through decompositions of categorical probability distributions. Suppose A, B, C and D are inputs and Z is an output. A structure written ABCD:ABZ preserves the joint input distribution and the relation of A and B to Z, while not retaining every dependence in the saturated distribution ABCDZ. Maximum-entropy composition of the selected projections yields a reconstructed distribution. Its conditional distribution can then be used to predict Z. The question is whether a simpler specified structure retains useful information about that output. [SG-W053]

The authors express predictive information as uncertainty reduction, for example H(Z) − H(Z|AB), relative to a stated independence model. Normalizing by H(Z) gives a percentage uncertainty reduction when that denominator is appropriate. This is a defined statistical comparison, not a measurement of energy saved or an automatic causal interpretation of a gene–gene relation. “Independence” also depends on whether the model is directed toward an output or treats all variables symmetrically. The model’s statement must be recovered before its simplicity or information score is interpreted. [SG-W053]

The method passages distinguish loopless feature-selection structures from models with multiple predicting components and loops. Loopless calculations are simpler and faster; loops can require iterative computation. Exhaustive searches over candidate structures can become prohibitive. Thus the representation may reduce a prediction problem while leaving model search, estimation and validation as substantial work. [SG-W053]

Ulanowicz and colleagues, 2009: what the flow identities say#

Ulanowicz, Goerner, Lietaer and Gomez begin with nonnegative directed flows Tij and total throughput T. Normalizing gives probabilities pij= Tij/T, together with sender and receiver marginals. Their joint entropy H describes uncertainty in the joint flow distribution; mutual information X describes statistical dependence between origin and destination. The remainder Ψ= H − X is the sum of the two conditional entropies, H(origin|destination) + H(destination|origin). It is not one conventional conditional entropy. These quantities require consistent support, a common logarithm base and the usual zero-probability conventions. [WME-W025, printed pp. 27–29]

Multiplying by throughput yields the paper’s extensive measures C = TH, A = TX and Φ= TΨ, with C = A + Φ. The authors call A ascendency and interpret it in terms of organized constraint or efficiency; Φ is

associated with overhead and reserve. The identity is an accounting decomposition of observed flows. An information-weighted throughput is not automatically a thermodynamic conversion efficiency, a stock of spare equipment, or a measured ability to recover after a disturbance. Those interpretations require further evidence about the system represented. [WME-W025]

The distinction between averages and individual events also matters. Independence maximizes expected joint entropy at fixed marginals. It does not maximize the surprisal of every possible joint event. An individual term log [pij/(pi·p·j)] can be negative, although the average mutual information is nonnegative. Statistical symmetry between origin and destination is not a causal version of Newton’s third law. The paper’s discussion of absence, constraint and autocatalysis supplies an interpretation of the equations; these broader philosophical assertions are not all consequences of the entropy identities. [WME-W025, pp. 28–29; WME-W024]

The food-web example and the conditions on fallback#

The 2009 article uses a simplified network involving prawns, turtles, fish, snakes and alligators. It isolates a small set of flows from a larger web and deliberately makes extreme changes for illustration. Concentrating modeled flow along fewer paths can increase the indicated throughput while removing an alternative route that would matter if fish failed. The example explains why a present flow arrangement and its apparent efficiency can fail to reveal a dependence on a particular intermediary. It is a didactic counterfactual, not an observed disturbance experiment. [WME-W025, p. 30]

The account retains the inputs, conversions and dissipation that sustain the organisms. Its reserve measure is expressly not a palpable stock. An alternative path is useful only if the organisms or facilities on it remain viable, have capacity, can perform a substitutable function, and can act within the disturbance’s time scale. Statistical diversity alone does not establish those properties. The authors also discuss autocatalytic concentration that entrains resources and can starve outsiders, so increased internal organization need not benefit everything affected by the network. [WME-W025; RGC-W042]

The proposed optimum and the critical preprint#

The article proposes a family of fitness functions of relative ascendency α= A/C, with a positive chosen parameter β. In natural-log notation, the normalized form is F = −e α^ βln( α^ β); robustness also includes throughput. The maximum at α= exp(−1/ β) follows from that chosen curve. It is not a theorem identifying the fraction that maximizes actual survival in every network. The article acknowledges limited data, a heuristic balance, a nonuniversal β, an arbitrary bound used in its illustration, and sensitivity to resolution. It leaves transfer from ecology to economics open. [WME-W025, pp. 31–35]

Trancón y Widemann and Bogner’s critical preprint targets the formal presentation. The preprint was submitted in 2012 and posted in 2016; rejection without review is the authors’ report, not evidence of peer-reviewed acceptance. The critics identify the two-conditional-entropy issue, problems with maximum-surprisal reasoning, ambiguity between source and receiver, and confusion between intensive information measures and throughput-scaled quantities. They expressly do not infer that the ecological observations or philosophical possibilities should be rejected wholesale. [WME-W024]

One consequential problem is the original use of dimensional A and Φ as exponents in transformed connectivity and effective-size measures. The intensive X and Ψ, with compatible logarithm conventions, are required for that construction. A further numerical example does not produce the printed relative ascendency from its stated connectivity and size. Nonnegative conditional entropy also does not establish that an entire network is connected: disjoint deterministic branches can satisfy the same inequality. [WME-W024; WME-W025, pp. 31–32]

The critical source itself requires discrimination. Its conversion between bits and nats is printed in the wrong direction: one bit is ln(2) nats, about 0.693, while one nat is about 1.443 bits. Loose statements about the convexity of logarithms also do not replace the relevant entropy argument. Local derivatives with respect to independently varied flows present another limit: real interventions may have to preserve mass balance and satisfy capacity, timing and material constraints. A sensitivity formula is not a feasible redesign procedure or a convergence proof. [WME-W024; WME-W025, eq. 18]

Goerner, Lietaer and Ulanowicz: the economic extension#

The related economic article argues that development depends on the organization and diversity of flows, not simply on aggregate economic throughput. It discusses enterprises at different scales, human capital, concentration, local circulation and the possibility that autocatalytic growth drains resources from surrounding participants. This is the authors’ proposed economic synthesis. [RGC-W042, pp. 76–81]

The transfer assumes that a flow framework can apply across matter, energy, information and money. The article does not supply a new measured economic network dataset with out-of-sample survival validation. Its comparisons of local spending and its historical interpretations of trade, banking, food control and empires remain attributed claims. Equity, human capability and the triple bottom line also involve normative commitments not derived from C = A + Φ. [RGC-W042]

There are additional quantitative discontinuities. A footnote identifies sustainability with capacity C, rather than the earlier throughput-scaled fitness function. C alone does not have that function’s claimed interior optimum in relative ascendency. The article specifies natural logarithms while repeating numerical information values from the bit-based example without the corresponding conversion, and a redrawn flow differs while the stated total remains unchanged. Calling mutual information “efficiency” and overhead “resilience” does not by itself measure energy efficiency or recovery. These defects limit the promise of a single established economic sustainability metric while leaving the conceptual argument available for study. [RGC-W042, p. 78 and fig. 2]

Ulanowicz, 2020: a narrower balance conjecture#

The 2020 article changes notation: A and Φ denote intensive quantities, and H = A + Φ. A reader carrying over the 2009 extensive meanings would misread the argument. The article reports 17 articulated networks clustering near relative ascendency 40 percent, selects an optimum of 0.401 with β= 1.0914, and explicitly expects optima in other domains to differ. This is a narrower empirical conjecture associated with a chosen fitness curve, not retrospective confirmation of a universal 46 percent rule. [RGC-W043, pp. 1–6]

Plotting networks on F( α) does not supply an independent measurement of their survival. Resolution, boundaries and which surviving systems entered the dataset matter. An oyster-reef example adds a hypothetical flow along a specified route; it is not an observed intervention. The printed derivative also loses a minus sign relative to the fitness function. Recommendations to adjust flows according to sensitivities still require a feasible physical intervention and do not establish dynamic convergence or real recovery. The article’s metaphysical claims about indeterminacy and life exceed what the entropy identities demonstrate. [RGC-W043]

These related articles and their criticism put explicit quantities behind a consequential structural proposal. [WME-W024; WME-W025; RGC-W042; RGC-W043]

Double contingency: expectations about expectations#

In the introduction and §9.1 of his 2021 chapter, Leydesdorff reconstructs the problem of an actor expecting how another actor will respond, while that other actor also forms expectations. An observed event and expectations about possible events have different roles: an expectation can orient a present selection before the anticipated event occurs. His chapter develops its own anticipatory modeling vocabulary from this reconstruction. [WME-W021, pp. 175–193]

Coordinated communication involves expectations about others, which differ from the existence of a network edge or an information total. For Leydesdorff’s related simulation account, see S49. [WME-W021, introduction and double-contingency exposition]

Funds, flows, connectivity and expectations#

The distinct units here remain explicit: time and maintained funds, supporting-energy accounts, incidence structures, categorical distributions, flow-weighted information, and expectations in a social model. Each source account identifies what its representation includes and what further interpretation it needs.
