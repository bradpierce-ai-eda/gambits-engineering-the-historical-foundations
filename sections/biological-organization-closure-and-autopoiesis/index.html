Several meanings of closure#

The selected sources concern systems that produce, constrain or maintain conditions of their own activity. The term closure has different meanings in the sources: dependence among constraints, a production organization, closure of a reaction set and mathematical conditions for self-maintenance. None of these means that an organism or chemical system needs no external matter or energy.

Montévil and Mossio: constraints and the processes they shape#

Montévil and Mossio distinguish thermodynamically open processes from constraints acting on them. A constraint is a contingent structure or dynamics that changes the process while relevant aspects of the constraint remain conserved over the time scale considered. Its presence alone is insufficient: the effect must differ from the unconstrained case. Their vascular-system example concerns oxygen carried through a structured transport system rather than simply diffusing in its absence. [W013; GL-W026, 2015 article, §§2–3, definition 1]

The time-scale qualification matters. Conservation of a relevant property during a constrained process does not mean the structure lasts forever or never needs repair. A relatively persistent arrangement can shape many faster events while its longer-term production and maintenance depend on other processes. This is how the framework avoids treating every constraint as a permanently external boundary condition.

The paper's example of an inclined plane clarifies the difference. The plane constrains a sliding object, but that sliding does not normally produce or maintain the plane. Biological organization, in their proposed account, involves constraints whose continued existence depends on processes constrained by others in the organization. The system uses constrained thermodynamic flows in maintaining the constraints themselves. [W013, §§3–5]

Dependence, boundary and closure are specified rather than inferred from a cycle#

The authors ask whether any circular chain is enough and answer by defining dependence more carefully. Their framework distinguishes direct dependence from an indirect contribution through a longer chain of processes. Strict closure requires the relevant mutual dependence among constraints, rather than merely reciprocal benefit in a broad environment. Constraints outside the closed set and specified boundary conditions can still be necessary. Closure is consequently contextual, while thermodynamic openness continues underneath it. [W013; GL-W026, §§4–6]

The upper and lower boundaries pose an explicit problem for the framework. Descriptions of actual organisms are incomplete. The authors propose considering maximally closed systems within the available description, then examining how interacting systems can retain distinguishable organization. Their hunting-and-cooking example treats some collective dependencies as indirect and warns that loose cooperation need not satisfy strict closure. A team doing mutually useful work is therefore not automatically a biological closed organization under their definitions.

The authors state that their purpose is to characterize general features rather than supply a model capturing all the complexity of real organisms. Their later discussion retains the relation between stability and variation. [GL-W026, §§5–7]

The abstract of Mossio, Montévil and Longo’s 2016 Theoretical principles for biology: organization proposes organization as a principle for making biological objects intelligible and relates it to variation. [GL-W091, abstract]

Corti: causal conditions do not by themselves settle what ought to happen#

The selected part of Corti's critique examines a counterfactual argument for biological normativity. In the organizational account as he reconstructs it, a constraint must perform its role or the mutually maintained system will cease to exist. A further inference assigns what the constraint ought to do. Corti questions whether the causal premise is sufficient for that normative conclusion. [W047, counterfactual argument]

His comparison with a simple dependence relation makes the issue concrete. A condition can be necessary for an outcome without thereby acquiring a normative obligation to produce it. He then asks whether making the conditions internally generated and mutually dependent changes that inferential step. He accepts that closure can mark an important descriptive difference while disputing that the difference, on its own, establishes intrinsic norms.

Dittrich and Speroni di Fenizio: chemical closure is a property of a reaction set#

Chemical Organisation Theory begins with molecules and reaction rules. A closed set contains the products generated by reactions available within it. This is a different meaning from a physical seal around a container. The theory also distinguishes semi-self-maintenance, in which consumed types can be produced, from the stronger quantitative condition needed to maintain amounts. [W077, author preprint dated 1 December 2006, §§2.2–2.3]

The authors' small reversible example is instructive. Two molecular types produce one another, but both also decay. Having a route that produces each type is not sufficient to keep either at positive concentration. A diagram of reciprocal production can therefore conceal net depletion. Their self-maintenance condition uses the stoichiometric matrix and the existence of a compatible flux vector for which the relevant net production rates are nonnegative. The stipulated positivity of available reactions is part of that definition.

A chemical organization combines closure and self-maintenance in this formal sense. Its existence identifies a structural possibility under the reaction rules and flux conditions. It does not, without further kinetic information, establish that actual concentrations will approach a stable persistent state. An available feasible flux is different from the flux generated by a particular reaction-rate law and initial condition. [W077, definitions 5–11]

The later selected theorem relates fixed points of the specified dynamics to organizations under a mapping from concentrations to present molecular types. Threshold and reaction-positivity assumptions enter the construction. The direction reported is that the appropriately mapped stationary state is an organization, not that every formally feasible organization is a stable attractor. [W077, §3.2, definition 41 and theorem 42]

Varela, Maturana and Uribe: a production organization that realizes its boundary#

The early sections of Varela, Maturana and Uribe's 1974 paper characterize an autopoietic system through a network of component-producing processes. The components participate in generating the network that produces them and in realizing the system as a unity in a domain. Its boundary is consequently part of an ongoing production organization, rather than merely a wall placed around a collection of parts. [W081, printed pp. 187–191, §§1–7]

The distinction between organization and a particular material realization allows turnover without making the system an unchanging stock of molecules. The selected model and figures illustrate the proposed organization. Their role is conceptual demonstration in a specified construction. Reproduction is also not identical to the maintenance of such an organization; a system can be described in terms of its organization without making reproduction the definition of each operation.

Marletto: a recipe requires an executing and maintained constructor#

Marletto's selected constructor-theoretic argument asks what tasks the laws of physics permit, including accurate construction and reproduction under laws that do not contain a ready-made design for the task. Possibility is defined through arbitrarily good approximate constructors short of perfection. This differs from asking how probable a task is under observed evolution, or how much a particular implementation costs. [W092, §§2–3.1 and selected conclusions]

Her car-factory example separates a task-specific recipe from a more general vehicle that carries out its constituent operations. The recipe can be decomposed into subtasks permitted by the laws, with a modular programme specifying their organization. The task-specific information must be instantiated and preserved; the executing machinery must also remain capable of carrying out the operations. A stored recipe is therefore part of an architecture, rather than an alternative to physical execution.

Marletto connects accurate constructors, replication and maintenance in an architecture that joins a reusable recipe to machinery capable of executing it. [W092]

Hopfield: specificity can require an additional driven path#

Hopfield's 1974 kinetic-proofreading paper asks how biosynthetic processes can discriminate more accurately than a simple equilibrium recognition scheme would suggest. In the model, correct and incorrect substrates differ in their dissociation behavior. An additional reaction stage, driven away from equilibrium, creates an effective delay before incorporation. The more rapidly dissociating incorrect complex has more opportunity to leave before the final product forms. [TA-W017, printed pp. 4135–4137, reaction schemes 1–10]

The driving is essential in his analysis. Within the equilibrium constraint, merely adding the intermediate does not supply the desired improvement. Coupling to an energy source, such as phosphate hydrolysis, changes the available kinetic organization. The effective delay is a physical reaction mechanism, not a decision by an external inspector. Reactions that appear superfluous in a simpler description can serve a discrimination function.

Hopfield relates the mechanism to reported biosynthetic examples. The model shows why fidelity can retain additional pathways and free-energy supply. [TA-W017]

Galstyan and colleagues: spatial proofreading relocates part of the work#

The selected spatial-proofreading account uses gradients and spatial separation to create a discrimination opportunity. A bound complex must persist long enough during movement or diffusion to reach the region where product formation occurs. Different dissociation rates can then yield different success probabilities. The arrangement supplies a spatially organized delay rather than requiring the same local chemical architecture as the classical scheme. [TA-W018, selected model and gradient-maintenance passages]

The authors make the boundary of energy accounting explicit. An initial reported power calculation concerns the additional cost caused by proofreading's tendency to homogenize a gradient; it excludes the baseline cost of creating that gradient. Appendix B separately examines the continuing power needed to

counter diffusion even without the proofreading enzyme. [TA-W018, energy-cost discussion and appendix B]

The more realistic model describes a membrane-associated kinase and a distributed phosphatase maintaining an active-substrate gradient. At low enough enzyme activity, the gradient can persist and support proofreading. If activity overwhelms the maintaining mechanism, the gradient flattens and proofreading is lost. The selected parameter examples use experimentally constrained ranges, but the study remains a conceptual model rather than a demonstration that every proposed biological implementation uses the mechanism. [TA-W018, gradient-maintenance model]

Spatial organization can alter where discrimination work occurs, while successful use still depends on maintaining the spatial difference.

Synthetic Notch: a selected modular sensing–response account#

The summary and introductory material from Morsut and colleagues describe synthetic Notch receptors with replaced extracellular sensing and intracellular transcriptional modules. Binding an external ligand triggers release of a regulator, connecting a selected input to a selected cellular response. Their account reports pathways that do not share common signaling intermediates and can be combined to integrate cues, including Boolean response programmes. [GL-W060]

The engineered sensing interface uses receptor processing, transcriptional machinery and cellular resources to execute its response. [GL-W060]

Hypercycles: cooperative organization with vulnerabilities#

The selected 2016 PLOS Topic Page defines a hypercycle as a cyclic autocatalytic arrangement of self-replicating molecular types, each catalyzing a successor. Its historical motivation concerns an error threshold: imperfect copying can prevent a long sequence from retaining the information needed for more accurate replication. Coupled shorter replicators were proposed as a way of organizing a larger informational capacity. [SG-W047]

The review retains difficulties in the traditional ordinary-differential-equation model, including parasites and limits to stable cycle size. It also distinguishes an experimentally reported cooperative ribozyme network from a hypercycle in the strict sense. [SG-W047]

Maintained organization, productive closure and fidelity#

The constraint framework describes a conserved causal role and mutual maintenance; chemical organization adds reaction-set and feasible-flux conditions; autopoiesis concerns component production and realized unity; constructor theory examines task possibility and recipes; proofreading makes fidelity and supporting work concrete.
