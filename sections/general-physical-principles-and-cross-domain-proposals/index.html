Physical and explanatory quantities#

The sources propose physical accounts and connections across physical, biological and cognitive domains. Physical free energy, variational free energy, entropy production, assembly index and reduced descriptive dimension concern different objects and operations.

Prigogine: thermodynamic openness and internal production#

Prigogine's Nobel lecture connects thermodynamics, kinetics, spatial and temporal structure, fluctuations and microscopic irreversibility. Its physical Helmholtz free energy is F = E − TS, using energy, temperature and entropy. This quantity belongs to the thermodynamic account; it is not introduced as a generic cost function for organizations or as the variational quantity in a later cognitive theory. [SG-W045, lecture opening, equation 1.1]

The entropy balance distinguishes exchange with the environment from nonnegative internal production. An open system can maintain or increase internal organization while exchanging matter and energy. That does not suspend the entropy balance over an appropriate boundary. A visually ordered structure is not enough to determine the whole balance, and an internal decrease cannot be treated as a global disappearance of entropy.

The expression of production through fluxes and forces uses local-equilibrium assumptions. The minimum-entropy-production result requires narrower conditions, including a near-equilibrium linear regime with appropriate constant coefficients. The lecture does not justify extending that result to every open steady state or every self-organizing process. These distinctions are central to what the physical source can contribute. [SG-W045, opening thermodynamic development]

Structure can appear while entropy production increases#

The Bénard-convection discussion provides an explicit countercondition to the idea that self-organization always minimizes production. Beyond a threshold, the system develops macroscopic flow structure while entropy production increases. The order of the pattern and the direction of the production change therefore answer different questions. [SG-W045, convection example, PDF p. 5]

Near equilibrium, thermodynamic stability arguments can use a restricted relation between fluctuations and restoring behavior. Farther away, kinetics and the sign of excess-production terms become important. An autocatalytic reaction can supply a necessary feature for instability in the setup being discussed without being sufficient for it. Naming positive feedback does not establish that a transition occurs or that its result is beneficial. [SG-W045, PDF pp. 6–9]

The relevant lesson is source-specific: the operating regime and reaction mechanism determine which result applies.

The Brusselator retains maintained reservoirs and reaction conditions#

Prigogine's chemical-clock model makes its supporting arrangement explicit. In the stated simplified reaction scheme, external species are held constant and rate constants are normalized. The homogeneous stationary concentrations are X = A and Y = B/A. Under the model's conditions, the threshold B > 1 + A2 marks instability of that branch and the emergence of oscillatory behavior. [SG-W045, selected connected chemical-instability development]

These equations do not describe an isolated chemical device sustaining itself without resources. Maintaining the external species is part of the model. Adding diffusion introduces possibilities of spatial pattern, with system size, shape and boundary conditions affecting what occurs. A repeating chemical pattern can be generated through the arrangement and kinetics without a central agent specifying every oscillation, while its material and energy provision remain.

The lecture also distinguishes behavior along a branch from selection among branches near a bifurcation. Fluctuations and history can matter to which continuation occurs. Its discussion of relative fluctuations retains the conditions behind large-number approximations; nonlinear reaction behavior and critical correlations need not follow the simplest independent-particle picture. [SG-W045, PDF pp. 10–15]

Irreversibility is an argument with restrictions and open questions#

The later lecture examines the relation between reversible microscopic descriptions and irreversible macroscopic behavior. Prigogine discusses alternatives involving representation and coarse-graining, then develops his own operator-based approach. The proposed transformation is conditional on properties of the dynamics and is not claimed to be possible for every periodic motion. [SG-W045, PDF pp. 16–21]

One apparent printed sign error in equation 7.8 conflicts with the nonnegative quantity defined earlier. The conclusion retains open questions and a proposal for relating levels of temporality. [SG-W045]

Bruers, Maes and Netočný: the variable and reversal symmetry matter#

The selected 2007 preprint studies entropy-production principles in linear electrical circuits through Langevin descriptions and dynamical fluctuation theory. It relates minimum and maximum principles to the close-to-equilibrium structure of fluctuations, including the parity of the relevant observable under time reversal. The choice between a current-like odd variable and an even variable is not an interchangeable labeling choice. [W079, selected setup and §§2.2–3.3]

The discussion explains a known counterexample and qualifications concerning unequal temperatures. Its conclusion questions exact extension beyond the linear regime and argues that dynamics, not thermodynamics alone, must justify further generalizations. These are results and interpretations within the selected circuit analysis. [W079, selected conclusion]

Bejan and Lorente: flow access is their proposed general principle#

Bejan and Lorente's selected constructal account proposes that finite flow systems persisting over time evolve configurations offering greater access to their currents. It treats branching flow arrangements across animate, inanimate and engineered settings as instances of a design phenomenon. The authors distinguish the proposal from a fixed final optimum or an inevitable end configuration. [W082, selected §§1–4]

Their engine-and-brake discussion also resists reducing every case to maximizing or minimizing one entropy-production quantity. Flow can involve useful delivery and dissipation in different parts of an arrangement. Their proposed generality therefore differs from the claim that nature always chooses the least resistance.

Bejan and Lorente extend their proposed law through scaling arguments. [W082, selected passages and conclusion]

Friston: variational free energy concerns a model of observations#

The opening and Box 1 of Friston's 2010 review present variational free energy in relation to a generative model and an approximate density over hidden causes. The formulation provides a tractable bound related to the surprise of observations, with perception and action interpreted through changes affecting that bound. Its energy and entropy terms are defined in that probabilistic construction. They are not simply the E and TS of the thermodynamic equation above. [W052, opening and Box 1]

Biehl and colleagues, and the response: a scoped mathematical dispute#

Biehl, Pollock and Kanai examine particular arguments connecting stochastic dynamics, Markov blankets and an inference interpretation. The selected critique identifies additional assumptions needed for some mathematical steps and gives counterexamples to a free-energy lemma taken at face value. It also questions the relation between a variational density and an ergodic conditional density, and notes that changing what parameterizes a density changes its interpretation. [W055, selected overview, observation discussion and lemma counterexample]

Their critique targets the mathematical arguments linking dynamics with inference, while leaving the wider free-energy programme open.

Friston, Da Costa and Parr's selected response distinguishes conditions on coupling, exact and approximate inference and flow on a manifold of conditional expectations. In their explanation, an average internal flow can be interpreted on a statistical manifold; an average flow on that manifold differs from each fluctuating internal trajectory. They defend the broader programme while addressing the assumptions highlighted by the critics. [W056, selected §§1–2 and 4–6]

The dispute concerns which assumptions support a Bayesian interpretation.

Assembly theory: a construction measure with specified building blocks#

Sharma and colleagues define an assembly space through elementary building blocks and permitted recursive joining operations. Previously assembled motifs remain available for reuse. The assembly index is the length of a shortest permitted construction path for an object; copy number adds information about how often a complex object occurs. The proposal links construction history, reusable structure and selection rather than describing complexity only by visual intricacy. [W084, selected printed pp. 321–325 and 327]

The measure depends on justified building blocks and allowed operations. A construction count is not automatically a person's cognitive burden or the full physical cost of making a molecule. The selected paper proposes empirical access, including molecular measurements.

Jaeger's selected critique distinguishes what assembly theory may characterize from what would be required to explain Darwinian evolution. [W085, selected discussion]

Constructor information: physical possibility includes alternatives#

Deutsch and Marletto's selected opening frames information through possible and impossible physical tasks. A constructor can perform a task repeatedly while retaining the ability to do so; a substrate undergoes the relevant transformation. The account emphasizes counterfactual alternatives: an actual state carries information in relation to other states it could have occupied. [W091, selected §§1–2, printed pp. 1–6]

The authors seek physical principles accounting for properties such as interoperability of information media. They explicitly present general principles as conjectures. The task-based formulation characterizes which repeatable operations are physically possible. [W091]

Wilson and Flack: a coarser description can have a specific explanatory role#

Wilson's renormalization-group lecture examines rescaling, fixed points and universality near critical conditions. Certain large-scale observables can become insensitive to some microscopic details under the relevant limiting description. The condition of being near the critical temperature and the class of system remain important. Universality is not an assertion that all physical systems behave alike or that their detailed design can always be ignored. [GL-W025, selected introduction and PDF pp. 10–11]

The account supplies an asymptotic physical explanation. Describing fewer relevant variables is different from showing that the actual system uses less energy or needs fewer controls.

Flack discusses endogenous coarse-graining: organisms or collectives can respond to relatively slow regularities, with feedback between aggregated descriptions and lower-level activity. The Santa Fe Institute's reconstruction presents this as collective compression and a possible account of downward influence. [TA-W041, through the Santa Fe Institute]

Multitime cognition and System 0#

Taniguchi and colleagues' selected quad-process proposal relates embodied dynamics, individual cognitive processes and language-based collective intelligence across time scales. It calls the collective symbolic layer System 3 and seeks to place it alongside Systems 0, 1 and 2. The account connects several research traditions and a philosophical interpretation of multiple rhythms of duration. [W051, selected §§2.2–2.4 and 3.1]

Collective predictive coding remains a hypothesis at this scope.

Haken, catastrophe theory and Bios: proposed transfers retain their prerequisites#

Haken's Can We Apply Synergetics to the Human Sciences? and his related 2017 article develop the conceptual account discussed in the shared-practice section. [W083, selected pp. 58–66; RGC-W038]

Golubitsky's selected opening to catastrophe theory distinguishes mathematical exposition from the difficulty of applying its assumptions. Classification of qualitative change in a mathematical setting does not automatically validate a social or biological application. Heuristics and proofs are kept distinct. [SG-W046, printed pp. 352–354]

Sabelli's Bios Theory of Innovation opens by relating recurrence and novelty within a proposed account of creative processes. The opening defers the empirical foundations of the proposal. [WME-W015, opening pp. 1–3]
