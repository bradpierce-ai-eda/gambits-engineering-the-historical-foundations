What supports a slab while it is raised#

The National Bureau of Standards investigation of the L’Ambiance Plaza collapse describes construction by lifting slabs. Floor slabs were cast near ground level, post-tensioned, raised by hydraulic jacks and secured to columns at the required elevations. During lifting, the jacking equipment and its connection to the slab carry loads that the final connection is intended to carry after attachment. The investigating report describes equipment, construction sequence and witness accounts as evidence from which to reconstruct the condition at collapse. [T10-W001]

How movement changes with excavation stage#

刘征 and 罗志琪 describe a large semi-top-down excavation near an existing 48-storey building. The staged work gave them reason to observe deformation while support conditions were changing. At point CX14 they report 38.9 mm at completion of the base slab and 48.4 mm at the later construction stage identified in Fig. 9. Most of the other points had final cumulative maximum displacements of 30 to 38 mm. The point, direction and construction stage matter to interpreting the values. The later reading concerns a different condition of the excavation and its supporting structure. [T10-W002]

Changing support during continued use#

The French engineering account of reinforcing ground below existing structures distinguishes treating the ground from transferring support through underpinning. Its Fig. 32 example uses temporary jacks and then sliding supports, followed by readjustments. The same account describes micropile arrangements for places where access is restricted. A transverse support can pass through a load-bearing wall and transfer its load to two vertical micropiles. What carries the load changes during the intervention. The example describes a sequence adapted to an existing structure and its ground conditions. [T10-W003]

The Federal Highway Administration’s bridge preservation guide connects inspection to decisions about maintenance and rehabilitation. It treats severity and the quantity of affected elements as relevant to the choice of action. Deterioration or loss of support can prompt more frequent monitoring, restrictions or closure. Its condition descriptions are institutional guidance for making those decisions. The excavation study instead provides measurements taken while a particular construction sequence was under way.
